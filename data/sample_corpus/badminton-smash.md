# Badminton smash mechanics

> Illustrative note written for this repository's demo corpus. It summarises general coaching principles. It cites no study and reports no measured data.

The badminton smash is a fast overhead stroke. Racket head speed at impact sets the initial shuttle speed. Players build that speed through a proximal to distal sequence: legs, hips, trunk, shoulder, elbow, forearm, then wrist and fingers.

Trunk rotation stores elastic energy before the forward swing. The hips open first and the shoulders follow. This delay stretches the trunk muscles and adds speed to the arm.

Forearm pronation and internal shoulder rotation contribute a large share of the final racket speed. A relaxed grip before impact allows a faster wrist action. Tightening the grip at contact transfers more momentum to the shuttle.

A jump smash adds a steeper angle. Landing control matters for injury risk. Coaches watch for a stable knee position on the landing leg.
