# Jump landing and knee loading

> Illustrative note written for this repository's demo corpus. It summarises general coaching principles. It cites no study and reports no measured data.

Landing from a jump creates a large ground reaction force over a short time. Bending the hips, knees and ankles spreads that force over a longer time and lowers the peak load.

Knee valgus, where the knee collapses inwards, raises strain on the knee ligaments. Coaches cue athletes to keep the knee in line with the second toe.

A soft, quiet landing is a simple field check of good force absorption. Landing on both feet spreads the load before athletes progress to single-leg landings.

Fatigue tends to degrade landing technique. Screening landings late in a session can reveal faults that a fresh athlete hides.
