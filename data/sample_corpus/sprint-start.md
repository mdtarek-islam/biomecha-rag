# Sprint start and ground contact

> Illustrative note written for this repository's demo corpus. It summarises general coaching principles. It cites no study and reports no measured data.

Sprint acceleration depends on horizontal force applied to the ground. In the first steps a forward body lean lets the athlete push backwards as well as down.

Block clearance uses a powerful extension of the hip, knee and ankle, often called triple extension. Arm drive balances the rotation that the legs create.

As speed rises, ground contact time falls and the body becomes more upright. Stiff ankles help the athlete return energy from each contact rather than absorb it.

Coaches track step length and step frequency. Speed equals their product, so a gain in one must not cost too much of the other.
