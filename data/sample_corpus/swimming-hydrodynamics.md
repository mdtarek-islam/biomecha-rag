# Swimming hydrodynamics and drag

> Illustrative note written for this repository's demo corpus. It summarises general physics principles. It cites no study and reports no measured data.

A swimmer meets three forms of resistance: form drag, wave drag and friction drag. Form drag depends on the frontal area the body presents to the water. A flat, streamlined body position reduces it.

Wave drag grows quickly as speed rises near the surface. Swimmers reduce it by staying underwater after starts and turns, within the distance the rules allow.

Propulsion comes from the hands and forearms pressing water backwards. A high elbow catch keeps the forearm vertical for longer, so more of the pull pushes water backwards.

Kick timing links the upper and lower body. In freestyle, a steady flutter kick helps hold the hips high and keeps form drag low.
